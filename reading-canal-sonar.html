The one idea

Brightness tells you which way the bed faces

The sonar on the boat sends a fan of sound sideways, to the left and right, 25 times a second. Each of these pings is one slice across the canal. The echoes come back in order of distance, near first and far last, and the app lays them out as a strip. How strong each echo is depends mostly on which way that bit of bed faces:

  • Facing the sonar, like the near side of a bump or the far wall of a hole: a strong echo, drawn bright.
  • Facing away, or hidden behind something: a weak echo or none, drawn dark. A hidden patch is an acoustic shadow.
  • Soft mud also echoes weakly. So dark on its own proves nothing; the shape around it does.

Echoes also weaken with distance, and depend on what the surface is made of. The app evens out distance by comparing every echo with the usual echo at that distance along the surrounding survey, so what stands out is mostly shape.

The sonar sits only about 0.9 m above the bed but listens out to 8 m, so the sound skims the bed at a low angle. That makes shadows long, and long shadows are what let small relief show at all.

Bumps

Raised objects: bright, then a shadow

Something standing on the bed catches the sound on its near face, which echoes brightly, and hides the bed behind it, which stays dark. Reading outward from the boat, a raised object is always bright, then dark, and its shadow points straight away from the boat's track.

The shadow's length gives the object's height, by similar triangles. This is the formula the app uses:

height h = H × L ÷ R H is the sonar's height above the bed, L the shadow's length, and R the distance from the boat to the shadow's end. In Fig. 2: 0.9 m × 1.0 m ÷ 6.0 m = 15 cm.

Turned around, it says how long a shadow to expect. Taller things cast longer shadows, and so does the same thing farther from the boat:

  • In the 3D model, each object gets a label such as raised 15 cm and a row in the Detected objects table. The Shadows switch marks its shadow in cyan, and the Relief colouring shows it red (higher than the canal's smooth shape).
  • In shallow water, sound bouncing off the surface partly fills shadows in, so they look dark grey rather than black. The app therefore judges darkness against the usual echo at that distance over the surrounding 30 m of survey.
  • Objects lower than 8 % of the water depth (at least 5 cm) are left out as too uncertain.
Depressions

Hollows: dark, then bright

A hollow reverses the order. Its near slope tilts away from the sonar and is partly hidden by the rim, so it is dark. Its far wall faces the sonar, so it is bright. Reading outward from the boat: dark, then bright. A scour hole or a missing lining panel looks like this.

  • Straight under the boat there is a second, direct measurement: the down-looking sonar measures the depth. A hollow the boat passes over shows as a measured dip in the Cross-section chart.
  • In the 3D model, hollows get labels such as hollow 14 cm, and the Relief colouring shows them blue (lower than the canal's smooth shape).
  • Gentle bumps and dips with no sharp edge have no shadow. For these the app reads slow changes in brightness instead: slopes facing the sonar echo a little more than flat bed, slopes facing away a little less. It adds them up into bed relief. This is an estimate; Bed detail from shading sets how much of it is used, and the Source colouring shows which parts were measured and which were reconstructed.
Cracks, steps and joints

The same rules, a few centimetres wide

Crack screening uses every sample of a short stretch: about 1.1 cm apart across the track, and one ping every 3–4 cm along it. At that scale a crack is a narrow hollow and a displaced panel is a small step, so the same rules apply.

  • A narrow crack and a step down look the same when the crack is too narrow for its far wall to show. The app then says crack, or a step down where the far side sits lower. Either way, the shadow's width gives a rough size, from the same formula as an object's height.
  • Joints: three or more lines across the canal at an even spacing are the joints between lining panels. The app reports them as joints, with their spacing. A joint that is out of line, much wider than its neighbours, or has a step at it is worth a look.
  • Direction matters. A line along the canal, parallel to the boat's path, is seen with the full 1 cm detail across the track, so cracks a few centimetres wide show. A line across the canal is seen with each ping's footprint along the track, 10–20 cm when 5–10 m out, so only wider gaps, such as joints, stand out.

Judging a find

How sure can you be?

The pattern fits the shape

Raised: bright, then dark. Hollow: dark, then bright. Shadows always point away from the boat, never towards it. A dark patch with no bright edge in front of it may just be soft mud.

It holds over many pings

A real edge is a clean, straight line over many pings in a row. Speckle, the grain of every sonar picture, changes from ping to ping.

It stays put on the ground

The boat weaves a little as it goes. A real feature stays where it is; a line that keeps the same distance from the boat is a sonar artefact, such as sound reflected off the water surface. The app removes these.

It is not a known edge

The bank toe and the waterline make long lines along the canal. Lines found close to the toe are marked may be the toe or a sediment edge and are never rated high.

The confidence rating

High: stands out far above the speckle. Medium: worth checking. Low: faint, and mostly speckle; hidden unless you ask for faint lines.

A second look agrees

Open the scan itself in the Sonar scan panel and the stretch in 3D. A second survey pass should show the feature again, mirrored if it was seen from the other side. Then check it on site.

What the sonar cannot tell you

  • Hairline cracks, and anything under sediment or vegetation.
  • Features smaller than a ping's footprint along the track, 10–20 cm when 5–10 m out.
  • Exact sizes: heights and step sizes come from shadows and are rough estimates.
  • Exact positions: they are only as good as the GPS, typically within a metre.

On the recordings of 7 July (about 390 m screened), the app found no high-confidence cracks. The few medium lines are short and unverified.
