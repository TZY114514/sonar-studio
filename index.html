Reconstruction settings

Transducer depth is how far below the water surface the sonar sits. Higher shadow sensitivity finds fainter objects, and more false ones. Bed detail from shading adds bumps and hollows read from echo brightness. Changes apply when you rebuild.

Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, and right-drag or use two fingers to pan. Click a numbered point or an object to show its scan and cross-section. The model covers the chosen stretch of route, centred on the selected point. Depth under the boat is measured by the primary sonar. The bed on either side, the bank slopes and the object heights are reconstructed from the sidescan: banks from where the echoes end (the waterline) and where they change character (the toe), objects from the length of their shadows, and bumps and hollows from echo brightness. Treat them as estimates. Not for navigation.

Screen a stretch to see it at full detail, with possible cracks marked

#WhatRunsLengthSizePositionAlongConfidence

No likely or possible cracks in this stretch.

The stretch is shown as the sonar saw it: along the route from left to right, port at the top, ground distance from the boat up and down. Drag and scroll to look closer; click the image to see that scan, or a line or row to select it. Lines that stand out of the sonar's speckle are marked: dark lines (gaps and shadows) and lit edges along, across or diagonal to the canal. Evenly spaced lines across the canal are reported as joints. The strip under the boat, the bank toes, the waterline and detected objects are not screened. GeoJSON downloads the listed lines. The size of a crack or step comes from the length of its shadow and is rough. Hairline cracks, and anything under sediment, cannot be seen by this sonar. These are possible cracks to check on site, not confirmed defects.
